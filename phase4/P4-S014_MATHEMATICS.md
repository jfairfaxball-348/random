## 1. Setup and the exact tail quantity

Fix the P4-S012/P4-S013 least-fresh scan generated by a self-avoiding partial rational stake functional B.

At a reachable epoch state s let j be the withheld least-fresh sentinel and let A_s be the computable avoidance tree from P4-S013. For a finite horizon H define

p(s,H) = 2^{-H} |A_s \cap 2^H|.

This is the exact conditional fair-coin probability, given s, that the epoch still has not triggered after H fresh filler answers.

Unlike P4-S013, p(s,H) is allowed to be positive for every H. Thus A_s may have infinite paths and the sentinel may be permanently avoidable on some siblings.

A horizon selector is a total computable function H(s) assigning a finite horizon to every reachable epoch state. Write

p(s) = p(s,H(s)).

### Definition — computably budgeted avoidance tails

The least-fresh scan has computably budgeted avoidance tails if there are:

1. a total computable horizon selector H(s); and
2. a computable rational B_0 < infinity

such that for every source run, if s_0,s_1,... are the successive epoch states actually reached on that run, then

sum_r p(s_r) <= B_0,

where the sum stops if some epoch never ends.

A stronger but easier-to-check sufficient form is: there is a computable summable sequence of nonnegative rationals epsilon_r with a known finite sum such that at every reachable r-th epoch state s,

p(s,H(s)) <= epsilon_r.

The proof below uses only the pathwise budget on the exact finite-horizon miss probabilities. It does **not** use a modulus for convergence of p(s,H) as H tends to infinity, and it does not require the eventual-trigger probability or any infinite-horizon optional projection to be computable.

This is the weakest effective summability condition established in this session. No claim is made that it is logically necessary for preservation or that no stake-weighted weakening exists.

## 2. A globally exhaustive sentinel-first completion

Define a physical completion C of the logical least-fresh scan.

At the start of an epoch with sentinel j:

1. physically query j immediately and store its fair bit b;
2. continue simulating the logical least-fresh epoch;
3. whenever the logical scan asks a fresh filler coordinate, physically query it and feed its answer to the simulation;
4. if the logical trigger becomes visible, append the stored b as the logical sentinel answer without making another physical query, then start the next epoch;
5. if the logical epoch never triggers, continue querying its successive fresh fillers forever.

### Lemma 1 — C is an effective adaptive permutation

C is total, computable and no-repeat. It queries every source coordinate exactly once on every source.

If an epoch never triggers, its fillers enumerate every coordinate other than its sentinel, while the sentinel was already physically queried at the start of the epoch. If every epoch triggers, each epoch physically consumes its least unqueried sentinel and finitely many fresh fillers; the same least-unqueried argument as in P4-S013 shows that every coordinate is eventually queried.

Therefore C is an everywhere-total computable fair-coin-preserving bijection with computable inverse, hence lies in the P4-S001 effective-isomorphism regime.

The important point is that C is exhaustive even when the original logical least-fresh scan retains genuine branchwise avoidance.

## 3. The miss-ticket martingale

Fix an epoch state s and its chosen horizon H(s). After C has physically revealed the sentinel, the event

E_s = "the logical epoch has not triggered after H(s) fillers"

depends only on the next H(s) fresh filler bits. Its conditional probability is exactly p(s). Self-avoidance is what makes the already-revealed sentinel bit irrelevant to this event.

Because A_s \cap 2^{H(s)} is a finite computable set, there is a finite fair indicator martingale ticket which costs p(s) at the epoch start and pays 1 exactly on E_s and 0 otherwise.

### Lemma 2 — one computable martingale catches infinitely many horizon misses

Start with reserve B_0. At every reached epoch state s, convert p(s) units of unused reserve into the fair E_s ticket. Keep all ticket winnings as cash.

The pathwise budget assumption ensures that the reserve is never overdrawn on any run. The resulting process J is a total nonnegative computable martingale on the completion stream.

After any finite number of resolved epoch tickets,

J >= B_0 - sum p(s_r) + number_of_misses.

Hence if horizon misses occur at infinitely many reached epochs, J is unbounded.

No independence assumption between epochs is used.

This direct ticket construction is why no Schnorr/ML effective Borel-Cantelli theorem and no computable sum of global event measures is needed.

## 4. Truncated exact hedging with restart

Let d be any nonnegative rational-valued computable martingale on the logical least-fresh output.

Construct a second computable martingale D on the completion stream C.

Suppose an epoch starts at logical transcript tau and D currently has capital K.

If d(tau)=0, keep D constant; d is then zero on every logical extension and cannot succeed on that branch. Otherwise put

alpha = K / d(tau).

Let H=H(s). Consider the bounded logical stopping rule:

- if the sentinel triggers within the first H fillers, stop immediately after the logical sentinel bit is appended;
- if no trigger has appeared after H fillers, stop after those H filler bits, before any sentinel consumption.

Its terminal payoff, multiplied by alpha, is a computable function of a finite completion block. By the same finite-tree conditional-expectation argument as P4-S009, its average is exactly K. Therefore D can hedge this payoff exactly during the first H fillers.

There are two outcomes.

### Good epoch: trigger by H

D ends the block with

D = alpha d(tau'),

where tau' is the logical transcript immediately after sentinel consumption. Thus D has tracked the full logical d-update, including the deferred sentinel wager, with no loss.

### Missed horizon: no trigger by H

At horizon H,

D = alpha d(tau_H),

where tau_H is the logical transcript after those H fillers.

After H, while the same logical epoch remains untriggered, D simply copies d on each further filler bit with the same scale alpha. These are ordinary fresh fair bits.

If the epoch never triggers, the logical output from then on consists only of those fillers. Hence if d succeeds there, D succeeds there.

If the epoch eventually triggers, the sentinel bit has already been physically revealed, so D deliberately **skips that one logical sentinel wager**: its capital does not change at the zero-physical-time logical sentinel step. At the next epoch start it recomputes the new positive scale K/d(tau') and resumes the same construction.

Thus each horizon miss costs at most one skipped deferred sentinel wager. No infinite-horizon conditional expectation is computed.

### Lemma 3 — finitely many misses are harmless

If d succeeds and only finitely many horizon misses occur, D succeeds.

After the last missed epoch, every subsequent epoch is good, so D maintains one fixed positive multiplicative scale of d forever. Removing a finite prefix does not affect unboundedness.

If instead a missed epoch never triggers, D copies d on every later logical output bit and therefore succeeds whenever d does.

## 5. One-martingale transfer

### Theorem 4 — computably budgeted tails force preservation in the least-fresh subclass

Assume computably budgeted avoidance tails. For every computable output martingale d there is one computable martingale M on the exhaustive completion stream C such that

d succeeds on the logical least-fresh output  =>  M succeeds on C(source).

Take, for example,

M = J + D.

If horizon misses occur infinitely often, J succeeds by Lemma 2.

If only finitely many misses occur and d succeeds, D succeeds by Lemma 3.

If a missed epoch never triggers and d succeeds on its infinite filler tail, D succeeds there as well.

Therefore M succeeds whenever d does.

By Lemma 1, C is a computable fair-coin-preserving effective isomorphism. Applying the already-settled P4-S001 transfer to M yields one computable source martingale succeeding on the original source whenever d succeeds on the least-fresh output.

Consequently every computably random source has computably random least-fresh output under this budgeted-tail hypothesis.

This proof does not compute the unbounded optional projection excluded by P4-S010.

## 6. The hypothesis is genuinely weaker than finite deadlines

Consider the self-avoiding zero-stake functional B defined as follows.

On input j, inspect coordinates j+1,j+2,... in order. Halt with stake 0 as soon as a 1 is seen. If all those bits are 0, diverge forever.

In the associated least-fresh process, each reached epoch has a genuine infinite avoiding sibling: the continuation with all future fillers 0. Therefore no epoch has a finite uniform deadline, and P4-S013 reachable-sentinel totality fails.

However, if the r-th reached epoch uses horizon

H_r = r+2,

then the exact conditional miss probability is

p_r = 2^{-(r+2)}.

Hence

sum_r p_r <= 1/2.

So the computably budgeted-tail theorem applies even though every epoch retains an infinite avoidance path.

This separates P4-S014 strictly from the P4-S013 finite-deadline regime inside the same least-fresh global-k=2 architecture.

The example is only a separation of hypotheses; its stakes are zero and it is not a randomness-destruction witness.

## 7. P4-S011 necessarily violates the budgeted-tail condition

Suppose, toward contradiction, that the exact P4-S011 least-fresh destroyer admitted a computable horizon selector H and a finite pathwise budget B_0 as above.

Apply Theorem 4 to P4-S011's computable output martingale d. It would produce one computable source martingale succeeding on the P4-S011 source Y whenever d succeeds on F(Y).

But P4-S011 proves that Y is computably random and d succeeds on F(Y). This is impossible.

Therefore the P4-S011 destroyer necessarily has **no** computably budgeted-tail certificate of the P4-S014 kind.

Equivalently: for every total computable horizon selector, the exact conditional miss probabilities cannot have one finite uniform pathwise sum bound over all runs.

This conclusion does not assert a fixed positive tail lower bound at each epoch. The failure may be distributed nonuniformly across reachable sibling states; what is ruled out is exactly the finite global budget needed by the miss-ticket martingale.

## 8. What is weakest here, and what remains open?

The proof needs only:

1. a total computable finite horizon H(s) at each reachable epoch;
2. exact finite-level miss probability p(s), already computable from A_s and H(s); and
3. one finite computable budget B_0 bounding the sum of those p(s) over the epoch states reached on every run.

A preassigned summable sequence epsilon_r is sufficient but stronger than necessary. No convergence modulus for H -> p(s,H), no computable infinite-horizon trigger probability, and no finite deadline are used.

The condition is not claimed to be absolutely weakest. In particular, this session does not settle whether one can replace unit miss tickets by stake-weighted tickets and permit nonsummable raw miss probabilities when the skipped sentinel wagers themselves have summable effective loss.

That is the smallest remaining quantitative refinement of this route.

## 9. Successes and failures

Successful:

1. Computably budgeted finite-horizon avoidance tails are sufficient for one-martingale transfer.
2. A single sentinel-first completion is globally exhaustive even on permanently avoiding siblings.
3. A fair miss-ticket martingale handles infinitely many horizon misses without any independence assumption.
4. A finite-horizon conditional-expectation hedge plus restart handles finitely many misses while avoiding any infinite optional projection.
5. The sum of the two martingales succeeds whenever the output martingale succeeds.
6. P4-S001 then yields one computable source martingale.
7. A concrete zero-stake example shows the condition is strictly weaker than finite deadlines.
8. The P4-S011 exact destroyer necessarily violates the condition.

Failed or deliberately not claimed:

1. No theorem is proved for arbitrary branchwise avoidance without a finite tail budget.
2. No absolute necessity of the budgeted-tail condition is claimed.
3. No stake-weighted nonsummable-tail extension is proved.
