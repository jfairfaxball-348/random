## A. A one-account theorem for TWO asynchronous, arbitrarily late infinite XOR masks

A **uniform late-start family** A_(t,i) consists of computable infinite bit sequences indexed by t in N and label i in a fixed nonempty finite set I, with A_(t,i)(j)=0 for j<t and a uniform algorithm for every bit. A second family B_(r,j) has independent start r and finite label set J. Put A_infty=B_infty=0. Each family can make INFINITELY MANY flips after activation; no finite-support assumption is made.

**Theorem A122-1 (INTERNALLY PROVED, precise restricted two-clock compiler).** From any total nonnegative rational output martingale d and the algorithms for these two families, uniformly construct ONE TOTAL nonnegative RATIONAL source martingale D (root d(empty)), such that for every source tail u, every t,r in N union {infty}, every labels i,j where relevant,

    d succeeds on u XOR A_(t,i) XOR B_(r,j)  =>  D succeeds on u.

The two activation clocks t and r need not be equal, bounded, decidable from u, or synchronized. A single account also survives ANY **finite** additional unknown set of XOR edits, even if the finite set and clocks depend noncomputably on u. These claims are about XOR masks, NOT arbitrary source-tree FOLD clocks.

**Explicit total rational construction.** For each clock assign p_infty=1/2 and p_t=2^(-t-2), t>=0; the weights sum to one. Divide p_t equally among its |I| labels (respectively |J|). Define, for s of length n,

    D_2(s) = E_(T,I,R,J) [ d(s XOR A_(T,I)|n XOR B_(R,J)|n) ],

where the two clocks and their labels are chosen independently and infinity means zero mask. This infinite-looking sum is EXACTLY FINITE RATIONAL at every n: group all T>=n and T=infty into one zero-prefix atom of weight

    a_n = 1/2 + sum_(t>=n) 2^(-t-2) = 1/2 + 2^(-n-1),

and likewise for R. Sum over T,R in {0,...,n-1,star_n}, expanding only the finitely many labels of activated clocks. The star_n option has zero mask through n. All terms, weights and masks are explicitly computable rationals. D_2(empty)=d(empty).

For every fixed complete XOR mask K, the function s |-> d(s XOR K|len(s)) is a nonnegative ordinary rational martingale: at each node the K next bit permutes the two children. Independent convex averaging preserves its exact martingale equation. The grouped finite formula equals that average at EVERY finite level, so D_2(s0)+D_2(s1)=2D_2(s), with no infinite-tail numerical approximation. For an actual fixed (t,i,r,j), its summand has fixed positive mass p_t p_r/(|I||J|), interpreting p_infty and absent labels correctly; hence for all n above both starts

    D_2(u|n) >= [p_t p_r/(|I||J|)] d((u XOR A_(t,i) XOR B_(r,j))|n).

The coefficient is independent of n. Infinite flips do not hurt this domination.

**Finite-edit composition A122-2.** Let epsilon_k=2^(-k-2); sum_k epsilon_k=1/2. Define the further finite sum

    D(s) = sum_(F subset {0,...,n-1}) (prod_(k in F)epsilon_k)(prod_(k<n,k notin F)(1-epsilon_k)) D_2(s XOR 1_F),  n=|s|.

For any ordinary source martingale D_2, this is a total rational nonnegative martingale by the independent-coordinate-flip child-average identity. Any fixed finite mask F has finite-level probability bounded below by (1/2)prod_(k in F)epsilon_k >0, since prod_(k notin F)(1-epsilon_k)>=1-sum epsilon_k>=1/2. Thus for every fixed finite F and clocks, D(u|n) eventually dominates one fixed positive multiple of d((u XOR 1_F XOR A_(t,i) XOR B_(r,j))|n). Empty F is included. There is ONE account, not a nonrational infinite convex sum or a sequence of finite-horizon capital functions.

To lift tail capital to keyed source x=a u, set D_source(empty)=D(empty) and D_source(a s)=D(s) for either key a. This is again an ordinary total rational martingale. Neither U, actual release times, the input-dependent masks, nor a stop price are needed to compute it.

**Corollary A122-3 (uniform compiler for all S121 infinite-tail release/erase maps).** For F_(U,h) of S121 choose one family A_(t,(a,b)) with zero prefix before t, bit a XOR b at t and h(a,t,j) for j>t; take the other family identically zero and F empty. Then the same single rational source account converts ANY winning output d to a source winner at EVERY corresponding x, including no-release sources (the positive infinity atom) and releases with infinitely many flips. It depends on h and d but NOT the c.e.-open set U. This upgrades S121's pointwise computable-homeomorphism argument to a uniform ordinary CR capital compiler and S120's finite-edit compiler to computably parameterized infinite edits. Pointwise computable XOR homeomorphism/finite-edit invariance also gives F_(U,h)(R_k) subset R_k for every fixed k. This does NOT claim all additive-two, all fair F2, or any arbitrary FOLD-clock compiler.

## B. CR outputs do NOT provide a uniform effective inverse selector

**Theorem B122-1 (INTERNALLY PROVED: CR-domain inverse/posterior obstruction).** Use S121's dense U with positive K and ordinary S120 G_U. There is NO oracle Turing functional that, on EVERY computably random output y, returns even ONE actual infinite source inverse of y. A fortiori there is NO uniform algorithm listing BOTH exact inverses (in any order), despite every fibre having exactly two actual paths and the map being total, globally fair and additive-use-one. Nor is there a uniform partial oracle functional defined on all CR y that outputs the *eventual* q_[0] value in {0,1/2,1}. These are totality-on-CR claims; individual sources x are Turing reducible to y nonuniformly as in S118.

**Proof independent of measure diagonal internals.** The only features used are U dense c.e.-open, K nonempty with SOME CR y, and the legal release/erase structure. Fix y in K intersect CR. Suppose a purported selector computes the FIRST source bit a at y after reading only a finite output prefix rho=y|N. Every y' in its CR domain extending rho forces exactly the same first selected bit a (determinism and oracle-use continuity). But since U is dense, there is a triggered continuation z extending rho, whose first trigger is at t>=N: earlier output-trigger decisions depend solely on the shared output prefix and y itself never triggers. At stage t the release decision depends only on the prior t emitted bits, so choose the released bit 1-a, not changing the trigger. The cylinder of output streams extending this trigger plus the specified release bit has positive lambda-measure and contains a CR output y'. EVERY genuine inverse above such y' begins with first key 1-a. This contradicts the selector.

For an algorithm producing BOTH inverses, at y in K the multiset of first input bits is {0,1}, determined after finite oracle use; in the nearby triggered CR cylinder with release bit c the multiset is {c,c}. Contradiction. For an algorithm computing the stabilized posterior, its output at y is 1/2 after finite use, whereas in the triggered CR cylinders the final value is 0 or 1. Contradiction. This is a continuity/topology obstruction on the CR subspace, not a claim that the posterior sequence q_[0](y|m) fails to stabilize (it DOES stabilize).

**Consequences B122-2.** Even positive-measure K (in S121 lambda(K)>=61/64), two CR sources, exact-two finite-fibre bound, additive-one use and CR-output posterior stabilization do NOT yield a selector or effective posterior limit uniformly on the CR domain. S121's no-computable-supported-measure desert and this nonuniformity are distinct: the latter holds for much simpler dense U with K intersect CR. For the diagonal K, density theorem gives many density-one points in K intersect CR, yet the above impossibilities persist. None of these negative certificates blocks the explicit mask compiler A122; indeed G_U conserves CR.
