## 1. Least-fresh stake scans

Fix a partial oracle functional B. Whenever B^Z(j) halts, assume it outputs a rational stake theta in [-1,1] and that the halting computation does not query oracle coordinate j.

The associated least-fresh scan is the P4-S012 process. At the beginning of an epoch, let j be the least source coordinate not yet queried and withhold j. Repeatedly query the least fresh coordinate other than j until a finite halting computation B(j)=theta is visible from the revealed oracle answers. At that moment query j and start the next epoch.

The scan itself is total even if B fails to halt: in a nontriggering epoch it simply keeps revealing fresh non-sentinel coordinates forever. Such a branch omits exactly the current sentinel and is the branchwise-avoidance geometry used by P4-S011.

Correctness of a predicted bit, or profitability of a stake, plays no role in the totality question below. Those properties matter only when constructing a destroying output martingale.

## 2. The exact weaker totality notion

### Definition — reachable-sentinel totality

Call B reachable-sentinel total for its least-fresh process if, for every oracle Z, whenever the process on Z reaches the start of an epoch with current sentinel j, the computation B^Z(j) halts with a valid self-avoiding stake.

No requirement is imposed on B^Z(n) when n is not a sentinel on the run generated by Z.

This is strictly weaker than full oracle-totality: for every Z and every n, B^Z(n) halts. The difference matters because filler coordinates may be queried before they can ever become sentinels.

### Definition — the epoch avoidance tree

Fix a reachable finite epoch state s and its current sentinel j. Continue the least-fresh filler rule while refusing to consume j. A finite binary string rho belongs to A_s if rho can be used as the successive fresh filler answers for |rho| steps and, after those answers, no valid halting computation B(j) is yet visible.

A_s is a computable prefix-closed binary tree.

### Lemma 1 — local totality, no avoiding sibling and finite deadline are equivalent

For every reachable epoch state s, the following are equivalent.

1. Every total oracle completion compatible with s eventually makes B(j) halt without querying j.
2. A_s has no infinite path.
3. A_s is finite.
4. There is a finite H such that every continuation triggers within H filler queries.
5. Such an H is computably searchable uniformly from s.

#### Proof

A completion on which the epoch never triggers gives an infinite path through A_s, and conversely an infinite path determines a compatible completion because the filler rule eventually asks every non-sentinel coordinate if the epoch does not trigger. Thus 1 and 2 are equivalent.

The tree is binary and finitely branching. By König's lemma, a finitely branching tree with nodes at every level has an infinite path. Hence no infinite path is equivalent to finiteness, giving 2 iff 3 iff 4.

For 5, search h=0,1,2,... and decide whether A_s has any node of length h. If A_s is finite, the first empty level is found. Conversely a found empty level is a valid uniform deadline. No external modulus is needed. QED.

This is exactly the compactness mechanism already isolated in P4-S009, now applied to the P4-S012 predictor/stake functional itself.

### Corollary 2 — full oracle-totality is sufficient

If B is total on every oracle and every input and remains self-avoiding, then reachable-sentinel totality holds. Hence every reachable epoch has a computably searchable finite deadline.

The full totality hypothesis is therefore sufficient, but Lemma 1 shows that the actual requirement is only totality on the current sentinel over the continuations of reachable epoch states.

## 3. Reachable-sentinel totality makes the scan exhaustive

### Lemma 3 — every coordinate is queried on every source

Assume reachable-sentinel totality. Then every epoch ends after finitely many fillers and its sentinel is consumed. On every source, every coordinate is eventually queried.

#### Proof

Each epoch is finite by Lemma 1. The sentinel is the least currently unqueried coordinate and is consumed at the end of the epoch. Any coordinate skipped by the increasing sentinel sequence must already have been queried as a filler. Therefore after finitely many epochs every fixed coordinate has been queried. QED.

### Corollary 4 — every fibre is a singleton

The least-fresh scan is no-repeat. By Lemma 3 it queries every source coordinate exactly once on every transcript. Therefore an output transcript assigns a bit to every source coordinate and determines a unique source.

Thus the least-fresh map is not merely globally k=2; under reachable-sentinel totality it belongs to the singleton-fibre k=1 regime inside that k=2 subclass.

### Lemma 5 — the inverse is everywhere computable

Given an output sequence y and a requested source coordinate n, simulate the scan from the empty transcript using successive bits of y as the answers to the scan's queried coordinates. By Lemma 3 the scan eventually queries n. Return the corresponding bit of y.

This computes the inverse on every y. Freshness gives fair-coin preservation exactly as in P4-S011/P4-S012. Hence the induced map is an everywhere-total computable fair-coin-preserving bijection with an everywhere-total computable inverse.

## 4. Preservation and the source-martingale transfer

### Theorem 6 — reachable-sentinel totality forces computable-randomness preservation

Let F_B be a least-fresh scan generated by a self-avoiding rational stake functional B satisfying reachable-sentinel totality. Then for every computably random X, F_B(X) is computably random.

#### Proof

By Corollary 4 and Lemma 5, F_B is in the k=1 effective-isomorphism regime proved in P4-S001. The inverse pair is everywhere computable and fair-coin preserving. Therefore SRC-0015 / THM-0038, exactly as used in P4-S001, gives computable-randomness invariance. QED.

### One-martingale reading

Suppose a computable output martingale d succeeds on F_B(X).

At each reachable epoch the deadline search in Lemma 1 halts. Therefore the finite conditional-expectation hedge of P4-S009 can be run for that block: reveal the sentinel early in a completion order, simulate the logical least-fresh scan until the bounded sentinel consumption, and hedge to exactly d's post-consumption capital. Concatenating these finite blocks gives one computable martingale on an exhaustive adaptive-permutation completion stream whose capital agrees with d at every completed epoch.

That completion is itself a computable fair-coin-preserving bijection with computable inverse. By the P4-S001 effective-isomorphism theorem, if the completion martingale succeeds then X is not computably random; equivalently, by DEF-0004 there is one computable source martingale succeeding on X.

So the deadline condition supplies exactly the missing effective transfer. No optional projection over an unbounded branchwise-avoidable stopping time is needed.

## 5. Full totality is strictly stronger than necessary

### Example 7 — a reachable-sentinel-total functional which is not fully total

Define a self-avoiding stake functional B by:

- on input 0, query oracle coordinate 1 and then output stake 0;
- on input 1, diverge on every oracle;
- on every input n>=2, output stake 0 immediately.

The first sentinel is 0. Its first filler is coordinate 1. Once that filler is revealed, B(0) is visible and the scan consumes sentinel 0. Coordinate 1 has therefore already been used as a filler and can never become a sentinel. The next sentinel is 2, and every later sentinel triggers immediately.

Hence B is reachable-sentinel total on every source, and the scan is exhaustive. But B is not total on input 1 for any oracle.

Therefore full oracle-totality is genuinely stronger than the preservation hypothesis identified here. The example only separates the totality notions; its zero stakes are not a randomness witness.

## 6. What is the weakest condition established here?

For this least-fresh architecture, the following are the same positive boundary:

- reachable-sentinel totality;
- absence of an infinite branchwise-avoiding continuation at every reachable epoch;
- finiteness of every reachable epoch avoidance tree;
- existence of a finite deadline at every reachable epoch;
- a computably searchable finite deadline at every reachable epoch;
- global exhaustiveness of the least-fresh scan.

The equivalence of the last two deadline formulations is special to the computable finitely branching avoidance trees: once finiteness is true, a deadline is found by finite search. Supplying a deadline modulus as extra data is therefore unnecessary.

This is the weakest effective totality/deadline hypothesis established for the exact compactness-and-finite-hedge transfer. It is not claimed to be logically necessary for computable-randomness preservation among all k=2 least-fresh scans. A scan may have branchwise-avoidable epochs and still preserve computable randomness for unrelated reasons. P4-S011 proves only that allowing branchwise avoidance is sufficient to permit an exact destroyer.

## 7. Sharp relation to P4-S011 and P4-S012

P4-S011 is excluded exactly where its construction needs freedom: on a sibling oracle, the current self-avoiding prediction may fail to halt forever. That gives an infinite path through the epoch avoidance tree, prevents any finite deadline, leaves the sentinel unqueried, and keeps the global fibre bound at two.

Target-only totality is therefore insufficient. P4-S011 already has totality and correctness along its computably random winning source, yet destroys computable randomness.

P4-S012's stake-level converse is unchanged. The present theorem says that if such a stake mechanism is promoted from target-totality to reachable-sentinel totality on every source, the resulting least-fresh scan ceases to exploit the k=2 ambiguity at all: every fibre becomes singleton and the map falls back into the settled P4-S001 regime.

No all-correct bit-autoreduction converse is obtained or needed.
