The P4-S015–P4-S031 bankroll/ticket sequence is no longer the automatic default trajectory. Its unresolved local questions remain available if they become essential to a deeper theorem, but the programme should not continue tightening that line merely because another local refinement is available.

## Sustained Phase-4 objective

The organising question is now:

> **What mathematical resource is exposed by the jump from injective observation to one binary degree of inverse ambiguity, and what else does that resource control?**

The k=1 versus k=2 phenomenon is to be treated as the beginning of a broader theory, not merely as one counterexample plus its local proof technology.

This is a sustained Phase-4 research pivot, not a one-session detour.

Phase 4 remains original mathematics on selected CAND-01. This pivot does not open Gate 4 or Phase 5, does not initiate publication or outreach, and does not alter PA-0001 or DEF-0020.

The previous session-local restriction to k=2 is lifted for future work only where the new finite-ambiguity questions genuinely require finite k>2, composition or comparison across k. No k>2 result is asserted by this pivot.

## Research axes

### 1. Robustness classes under bounded ambiguity

Develop

R_k = {x in CR : F(x) is in CR for every total computable fair-coin-preserving F with every fibre of size at most k}.

Use the settled results as the base facts:

- R_1 = CR follows from P4-S001.
- P4-S011 supplies a computably random source outside R_2, so R_2 is a proper subclass of CR.

These are internal mathematical consequences of settled programme results, not novelty claims.

Investigate the hierarchy R_2,R_3,..., possible collapse, robustness under all finite multiplicities, intrinsic source-side descriptions, and links with self-avoiding predictability / autoreducibility.

### 2. The true in-between structure

Search for structural hypotheses strictly between injectivity and bare global two-to-one-ness which determine preservation versus failure.

Candidate axes include finitely many double fibres, effectively null ambiguity, exactly-two-to-one a.e. behaviour, effective or partial inverse selectors, computably distinguishable branches, bounded ambiguity-resolution delay, finite dependency frontiers, local effective invertibility, lower branch-weight bounds, vanishing secondary weights, finitely recurrent ambiguity, and one global unresolved binary choice versus renewable ambiguity.

The goal is a threshold theorem or invariant, not a catalogue of examples.

### 3. Reverse the direction

Study whether finite ambiguity can erase exploitable nonrandomness or map a non-computably-random source to a computably random output, and separate forward conservation from randomness creation / no-randomness-from-nothing phenomena at the individual-sequence level.

Seek exact constructions, impossibility results and converses.

### 4. Compare randomness notions

Use finite-ambiguity maps as probes for Martin-Löf, Schnorr, computable and Kurtz randomness, computable-selection / Church-stochastic notions, nonmonotonic/Kolmogorov-Loveland-style notions where exact definitions permit, and quantitative notions such as effective dimension.

Prefer a small number of sharp comparative theorems over a survey.

### 5. Composition, factorisation and an ambiguity budget

Investigate binary-stage composition, effective decomposition of finite-to-one maps, whether robustness under every binary stage controls arbitrary finite multiplicity, whether log_2 fibre size captures anything real, and whether repeated temporal ambiguity is more important than static cardinality.

A factorisation theorem or obstruction is a high-value target.

### 6. Identify the actual resource

Test candidate explanations suggested by the settled mathematics: hidden inverse information, sibling partiality, self-avoiding prediction, nonuniform inverse selection, delayed revelation, renewable unresolved binary choices, and failure to aggregate branchwise betting advantages into one computable source martingale.

Seek an invariant or criterion explaining both k=1 safety and k=2 failure. If a better parameter emerges, allow it to replace k as the organising variable.

### 7. Mutate the P4-S011 machine

Systematically alter the construction while tracking total computability, fair-coin preservation, fibre bound, source randomness, output randomness and inverse information.

High-value mutations include putting the exceptional source on a double fibre, forcing all fibres to have size two, recurrent/migratory/symmetric ambiguity, extra regularity or shift-like structure, arbitrarily weak/sparse prediction advantage, interacting sentinels under a small fibre bound, and renewable ambiguity patterns.

### 8. Look for converses

Investigate whether vulnerability of a computably random x to a total computable fair-coin-preserving global-k=2 map forces source-side structure such as partial self-avoiding prediction, autoreduction or a related mechanism.

A converse for a natural subclass would materially upgrade P4-S011 from a construction to a characterization framework.

## Programme-management rule

P4-S032 is the first reconnaissance/theorem-selection session of this branch.

It should perform enough exact mathematics across several axes to distinguish deep routes from superficial ones, then select the strongest theorem target for sustained follow-up. It should not try to solve every axis in one session.

Subsequent sessions may stay on the selected theorem target for multiple sessions if the mathematics warrants it. They should not automatically return to the P4-S015–P4-S031 ticket/reserve sequence unless the chosen deeper problem requires that machinery.

The decision criterion is no longer "smallest unresolved local lemma". It is "deepest exact theorem target that best explains, generalises, characterises or exploits the one-bit inverse-ambiguity phenomenon."
