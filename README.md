# Fairfax-Ball Randomness Research Programme

This repository is the authoritative research record for a five-phase programme whose long-term goal is to determine whether there is a mathematically natural, genuinely novel and publishable notion worthy of the name **Fairfax-Ball Randomness**, and, only if earned by the mathematics, to define it rigorously and place it in the public mathematical record.

P4-S001 settles k=1 positively. P4-S002 shows that k=2 does not force total inverse branches. P4-S003 adds uniform two-prefix inverse lists and a positive theorem under an explicit effective sheet split. P4-S004 isolates the low-weight stopping/coherence problem. P4-S005 refutes the direct effectivization route: even a k=2 map with a computable clopen injective-sheet split can have a noncomputable low-weight crossing measure, and the natural symmetric fibre-count lift can have noncomputable total mass. P4-S006 then shows that the canonical lexicographic split is only effective Borel/Baire-1 in general and can have noncomputable component masses; a single rotating hidden bit also retains the filler pre-revelation obstruction. P4-S007 further shows that delayed coalescence admits a coherent computable width-two skeleton with fixed-precision finite injury, while generalized post-coalescence collapse forces any scan-style witness to keep future betting positions fresh relative to a global computable coalescence schedule. This still does not give non-conservation; general k=2 preservation/failure remains unresolved at the freshness boundary. P4-S008 then rules out the persistent-hole scan route: any fixed omitted coordinate yields a total computable adaptive permutation completion, so a computably random winning scan source would have to lie on a singleton fibre and query every coordinate eventually. P4-S009 sharpens the surviving route: an unavoidable sentinel has a computably searchable finite deadline and its deferred wager can be hedged exactly, so the only hard turnovers are consumed on the target while omittable on a sibling continuation. Finite-horizon savings/restart supplies no rate-free infinite-turnover guarantee; no exact destroyer or full scan-preservation theorem is obtained. CAND-02 is not selected; CAND-03 remains retired.

The next bounded task is P4-S010, still restricted to k=2, focused only on the branchwise-avoidable deferred-wager case isolated by P4-S009: determine whether threshold-capping makes the eventual-consumption/threshold value computable enough for one optional-projection martingale, or construct an exact adaptive singleton-spine global k=2 witness with all global checks. ## Five gated phases

1. **Research / Catalogue** — build an extensive, source-grounded, agent-searchable map of algorithmic randomness and adjacent foundations. External papers need not be stored; catalogue metadata, exact theorem scope, links, notes and derived research records are stored.
2. **Discovery** — use the Phase-1 library to identify open questions, structural gaps and a bounded portfolio of serious candidate contributions.
3. **Novelty / Prior Art** — stress-test candidates for prior discovery, mathematical interest, usefulness, beneficiaries and publication relevance. No candidate enters mathematics without passing this gate.
4. **Mathematics** — perform the proof/disproof work on the selected target. Formalisation in Lean and Palomar registration may be included when appropriate, but do not confuse formal verification with novelty or significance.
5. **Publication** — prepare an arXiv preprint and pursue an appropriate peer-reviewed journal publication, with accurate disclosure, source checking and external-review discipline.

A phase transition is permitted only when the current phase's committed gate record is marked **PASS** and the authoritative state explicitly opens the next phase.

## Long-term objective

The name **Fairfax-Ball Randomness** is an objective of the programme, not a presupposed result. The programme must be willing to conclude that a proposed notion is already known, equivalent to an existing notion, mathematically uninteresting, inadequately motivated, or otherwise not publication-worthy. The name is earned only by a definition and theorem package that survive the preceding gates.
